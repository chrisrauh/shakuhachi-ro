# Koga fingering inventory (draft for review)

All 84 fingerings from Koga's fingering chart (*Koga II*, pages 107–108), checked against *Koga I*, pages 37 and 39, and *Koga II*, page 109. Source photos are in this folder. ⚠ marks a reading that is still uncertain. Community review of these readings is tracked in #423.

## How to read it

- **Pitch:** the written note for a standard 1.8 shakuhachi in D. Koga prints a note above only the first column of each group. A column without a note is an alternative fingering for the pitch of the nearest noted column before it, even across a row break (#22 belongs to B4, and #64–65 to D♭6).
- **Holes:** in the chart's order, top to bottom: back (thumb) hole 5, then front holes 4, 3, 2, 1, with 1 nearest the bottom of the flute.
  - ● closed
  - ○ open
  - ◐ half covered
  - ◕ mostly covered
  - ◔ slightly covered

  The photos show how much of a hole is covered only roughly. Read the partial symbols as approximate.
- **Name:** written as in the chart, e.g. ツメ for tsu no meri. "—" means Koga gives no name for that fingering.
- **Chin:** how far to lower or raise the head. "Meri" lowers it and "kari" raises it; "neutral" is neither. The degree of meri comes from the name: chu-meri is slight, meri is more, dai-meri is most. In brackets is where each value comes from:
  - **p37 / p39:** Koga's "Meri" or "Kari" label on those pages, or no label (neutral). The pages give no degree.
  - **name:** implied by the name, where pages 37 and 39 don't cover the fingering.
  - **inferred:** the same holes as a named fingering, but a semitone lower or higher, so the chin must make up the difference. This is my reasoning, not Koga's.
  - **?:** Koga gives no information.

  Holes alone can't identify a fingering. For example, ro dai-meri, ro meri and ro all use ●●●●● and differ only in the chin.

## Observations for the table design

- **45 of the 84 fingerings have a name (44 on p107–108, plus san no u from p39); the other 39 are unnamed alternatives.** Names are needed only for the named ones. The unnamed ones can still be stored as alternative holes for their pitch, for fingering diagrams later.
- **The name is the key, and holes and chin are attached data.** Same holes, different pitch:
  - #1–3: ro dai-meri, ro meri, ro;
  - #22–23: B4 and ri;
  - #50 and #53: A5 and san no u.
- **Chin can't be derived from the name.** Koga marks u "Meri" (#12), and san no u and shi no ha "Kari", though none of these names says so. So the table needs its own chin field.
- **Schools name the same fingering differently.** Nyokai-An calls ●●●◐◐ (F) re meri, where Koga calls it re dai-meri. Ro shifts the same way. Names follow Koga.
- **Koga differs from the current table** (`kinko-pitch-map.ts`). Koga is canonical, so these change:
  - B♭4 is ri meri (#19), not chi meri.
  - B♭5 is hi meri (#52), and B5 is hi chu-meri (#56), not chi chu-meri and ri.
